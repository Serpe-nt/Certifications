<Problem>
The shop's frontend is slow, and the incident channel asks three questions at once: how many requests is it getting, how many fail, and how long do they take? Nobody has a dashboard for this service yet. You have Explore and a request histogram, and you should not need to search the web for the queries.
</Problem>

<Idea>
Three queries describe any request-driven service: the request rate, the share of requests that fail, and a percentile of the duration. All three come from one request histogram. The rate and the errors come from its `_count` series, and the duration from its `_bucket` series, each read through `rate()` and aggregated as this module taught.
</Idea>

## One histogram, three answers

Module m02 called these three numbers RED: rate, errors, duration. The shop's services are instrumented with OpenTelemetry and send their metrics to Mimir. HTTP servers record every request in a histogram named `http.server.request.duration`, which Mimir stores as `http_server_request_duration_seconds` with `_bucket`, `_sum` and `_count` series. Each series carries the service's name in `service_name` and the response's status in `http_response_status_code`.

The `_count` series is a counter of requests, as step 7 explained, so you never need a separate request counter.

## Rate: requests per second

Take the rate of the count and add up all series of the service:

<Query lang="promql">{`sum(rate(http_server_request_duration_seconds_count{service_name="frontend"}[5m]))`}</Query>

Rate first, then sum, as in step 3. Add `by (http_request_method)` or another label if you want the rate split.

## Errors: failed requests as a share

Divide the rate of failed requests by the rate of all requests. The OpenTelemetry conventions count a server's response as an error when its status code is in the 5xx range; a 4xx is the client's mistake, not the server's:

<Query lang="promql">{`(sum(rate(http_server_request_duration_seconds_count{service_name="frontend", http_response_status_code=~"5.."}[5m])) or vector(0)) / sum(rate(http_server_request_duration_seconds_count{service_name="frontend"}[5m]))`}</Query>

Both sides are summed to no labels, so they match without `on`. The `or vector(0)` gives 0 instead of an empty result while no 5xx series exists, the case of step 6. Sum the numerator and the denominator before you divide, never average ratios, as step 12 showed.

## Duration: a percentile from the buckets

The 95th percentile over five minutes, for all pods of the service:

<Query lang="promql">{`histogram_quantile(0.95, sum by (le) (rate(http_server_request_duration_seconds_bucket{service_name="frontend"}[5m])))`}</Query>

Keep `le` in the `by` clause, as step 7 showed. The OpenTelemetry conventions advise 14 bounds for this histogram, from 0.005 to 10 seconds. With `+Inf` that makes the 15 buckets you find in the lab, and the estimate is precise only to the bucket it lands in.

<Flow caption="Three queries from one histogram">
  <Stage title="_count" kicker="Rate">sum(rate(x_count[5m]))</Stage>
  <Stage title="_count with 5xx" kicker="Errors">sum of failed rate / sum of all rate</Stage>
  <Stage title="_bucket" kicker="Duration">histogram_quantile(0.95, sum by (le) (rate(x_bucket[5m])))</Stage>
</Flow>

## The same pattern on any service

Only the names change from service to service. Services that speak gRPC record `rpc_server_call_duration_seconds` instead. Tempo also computes span metrics from every trace and writes them to Mimir: `traces_spanmetrics_calls_total` counts spans by `service_name`, `span_kind` and `status_code`, and `traces_spanmetrics_latency_bucket` holds their durations. That gives RED for every traced service, which is what the dashboard Shop / checkout (RED) from module m02 shows.

<Question id="q-m04-s13" answer="b">
A gRPC service records `rpc_server_call_duration_seconds` as a classic histogram. Which query gives its request rate?

- `sum(rate(rpc_server_call_duration_seconds_sum[5m]))`

  The rate of the sum is seconds of work per second, not requests per second.

- `sum(rate(rpc_server_call_duration_seconds_count[5m]))`

  The _count series counts one per request, so its rate is the request rate.

- `sum(rpc_server_call_duration_seconds_count)`

  This is the total since the processes started, not a rate.

- `histogram_quantile(0.5, sum by (le) (rate(rpc_server_call_duration_seconds_bucket[5m])))`

  This is the median duration, not the number of requests.
</Question>
