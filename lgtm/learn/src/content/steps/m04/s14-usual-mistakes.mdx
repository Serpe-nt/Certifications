<Problem>
A colleague's dashboard shows CPU use of 358,000 for a node with four CPUs, an empty latency panel, and an error rate that looks too low. No panel shows an error. Each one runs a query that Prometheus accepted, and each query is wrong in a way you met earlier in this module.
</Problem>

<Idea>
Most PromQL mistakes do not fail loudly. A counter read without `rate()`, a sum taken before the rate, a quantile without `le`, an average of ratios or two vectors with different labels all return a number or an empty result. Knowing the handful of usual slips, and the symptom each one leaves, is faster than debugging from scratch.
</Idea>

## Wrong numbers that look fine

These queries return values, and nothing warns you:

- A counter without `rate()`. `sum by (instance) (node_cpu_seconds_total)` adds CPU seconds since boot, a number that only grows. Wrap the selector in `rate()`.
- `rate()` of a gauge. `rate(node_load1[5m])` reads every fall as a reset. Use the gauge's value, `deriv()` or an `_over_time` function.
- The sum before the rate. A sum of counters drops when one pod restarts, and `rate()` cannot tell that from a reset. Write `sum(rate(x[5m]))`.
- The average of ratios. `avg()` of per-node ratios gives a small node the same weight as a big one. Sum the numerators, sum the denominators, then divide. The same is true of averaging the quantiles of several summaries, which the documentation calls statistically nonsensical.

<Compare caption="The same question, wrong and right">
  <Col title="Wrong">sum by (instance) (node_cpu_seconds_total): seconds since boot, hundreds of thousands per node in the lab.</Col>
  <Col title="Right" hot>sum by (instance) (rate(node_cpu_seconds_total[5m])): CPU seconds per second, about 4 on a worker.</Col>
</Compare>

## Empty results

An empty result is easy to read as "nothing is happening". It often means that the query matched nothing:

- `histogram_quantile()` after `sum` without `le`. The buckets collapse, and the function has nothing to read. Write `sum by (le)`.
- Two vectors whose labels differ. `a / b` pairs only series with the same labels. Add `on (...)` or `ignoring (...)`, and `group_left` when one side has more series.
- A regular expression without wildcards. `job=~"node"` must match the whole value. Write `job=~"node.*"`.
- A window shorter than two samples. `rate(x[30s])` with a 30-second scrape interval has nothing to compute from. Use a window of several scrape intervals.
- A classic bucket bound that does not exist. `x_bucket{le="0.25"}` matches nothing when the buckets are 0.2 and 0.4.

## Errors, and how to read them

Some mistakes are refused, and the message points at the fix:

- "ranges only allowed for vector selectors": a `[5m]` after an expression. Move the range onto the selector, or use a subquery `[5m:]`.
- "offset modifier must be preceded by an instant vector selector or range vector selector or a subquery": `offset` after a function. Put it right after the selector.
- "vector selector must contain at least one non-empty matcher": every matcher accepts the empty string. Add a name or a matcher that needs a value.
- "found duplicate series for the match group": the `on (...)` labels do not make each series unique on one side. Match on more labels, or add `group_left`.

<Trap practice>
If query 5 still returns a value in the thousands, `rate()` is missing or wraps the sum. It must sit directly on `node_cpu_seconds_total[5m]`, inside `sum by (instance)`.
</Trap>

<Remember>
When a query gives a strange number, check for a counter without `rate()`, a gauge with `rate()`, a sum before the rate or an average of ratios. When it gives nothing, check `le`, the labels on both sides, the regular expression and the window.
</Remember>

<Question id="q-m04-s14" answer="d">
`errors:ratio_rate5m` holds one error ratio per pod. One pod serves 1,000 requests a second with 1% errors, nine pods serve 10 each with 10% errors. A panel shows `avg(errors:ratio_rate5m)`. What is wrong?

- Nothing; avg() of the ratios is the service's error ratio

  The average is 9.1%, but the service as a whole fails about 1.7% of its requests: 19 errors in 1,090. The busy pod should count more.

- The panel needs max() instead of avg()

  max() shows the worst pod, 10%. That is a different question, not the service's ratio.

- avg() cannot be applied to recorded series

  Aggregation operators work on any instant vector, recorded or not.

- Ratios must be aggregated as a sum of errors divided by a sum of requests

  Summing the numerators and the denominators first weights each pod by its traffic, which gives the real ratio.
</Question>
