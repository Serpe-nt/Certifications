<Problem>
You know the request rate of the server right now. The capacity review asks for something else: the highest request rate of the last hour. `max_over_time()` needs a range vector, but `sum(rate(...))` is an instant vector, and the brackets of step 1 only follow a selector. Writing `sum(rate(...))[1h]` is refused.
</Problem>

<Idea>
A subquery evaluates an instant query again and again over a range, at a step you choose, and returns the results as a range vector. That range vector can go into any `_over_time` function. The syntax is the range and the step in square brackets, separated by a colon: `[1h:1m]`.
</Idea>

## From an expression to a range vector

Step 5 applied `_over_time` functions to raw samples, such as `node_load1[1h]`. A subquery lets you do the same with any expression:

<Query lang="promql">{`max_over_time(sum(rate(prometheus_http_requests_total[5m]))[1h:1m])`}</Query>

Read it from the inside. `sum(rate(...[5m]))` is the server's request rate at one moment. `[1h:1m]` evaluates it every minute over the last hour, which gives 60 values. `max_over_time` returns the largest of them. Without the colon, `[1h]` would mean a range selector, and Prometheus refuses it after an expression with "ranges only allowed for vector selectors".

You can also run a subquery on its own in the Table tab. `sum(rate(prometheus_http_requests_total[5m]))[30m:5m]` lists six values, one every five minutes.

## Range and resolution

The part before the colon is the range: how far back. The part after it is the resolution: the step between evaluations. The resolution may be left out, as in `[1h:]`. Prometheus then uses the global evaluation interval, which is 30 seconds in the lab.

The resolution decides both the cost and the detail. `[1h:1m]` evaluates the inner query 60 times; `[1h:10s]` evaluates it 360 times and catches shorter peaks. The inner query keeps its own window: `rate(...[5m])` inside `[1h:1m]` still averages over five minutes at every step.

<Flow caption="How a subquery is evaluated">
  <Stage title="Inner query" kicker="Instant vector">sum(rate(x[5m])) at one moment.</Stage>
  <Stage title="[1h:1m]" kicker="Range vector">Evaluated every minute for an hour: 60 values.</Stage>
  <Stage title="max_over_time()" kicker="Instant vector">The largest of the 60 values.</Stage>
</Flow>

## When not to use one

Subqueries are convenient but can be costly: the inner query runs once per step, and a dashboard runs the whole query again at every refresh. The query examples say that using subqueries unnecessarily is unwise. When a dashboard or an alert needs the same subquery all the time, record the inner expression with a recording rule, as step 12 shows, and apply `max_over_time` to the stored series instead. That is a plain range selector again, with no subquery.

<InLab>
The lab's Prometheus has `evaluation_interval: 30s`, so a subquery without a resolution, such as `[1h:]`, evaluates its inner query every 30 seconds.
</InLab>

<Trap>
`[5m]` after a selector and `[5m:]` after an expression look alike but do different things. The first fetches stored samples; the second evaluates a query at steps. Use the colon only when the inner part is not a plain selector.
</Trap>

<Remember>
`expression[range:resolution]` evaluates any instant query over a range and gives a range vector for the `_over_time` functions. Leave out the resolution and the evaluation interval is used.
</Remember>

How many times is the inner query evaluated in `max_over_time(rate(errors_total[5m])[2h:10m])`?

- 24 times, one for every 5 minutes of the 2 hours

  5m is the window of rate() inside the subquery. It does not set the step.

- 12 times, one for every 10 minutes of the 2 hours

  The resolution after the colon sets the step, so two hours at ten minutes give 12 evaluations.

- Once, because max_over_time() takes one value

  max_over_time() needs a range of values. The subquery produces them by evaluating many times.

- 120 times, one for every minute of the 2 hours

  The step is the resolution of 10m, not one minute.
