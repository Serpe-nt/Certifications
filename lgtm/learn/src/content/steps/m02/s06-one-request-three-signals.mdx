<Why>
You now know three signals, but only as ideas. In a real incident you jump between them fast: a graph shows a slow minute, you need one slow request from that minute, then the log lines it wrote. If you have never made those jumps, the first time should not be at 3 a.m.
</Why>

<Idea>
The lab links the three signals by one value: the trace ID. A latency graph carries example trace IDs, called exemplars. A trace links to the logs that carry its trace ID, and each of those log lines links back to the trace.
</Idea>

<Points>
  <Point label="Metric to trace: exemplars">An exemplar is one real trace kept next to a metric value. Grafana shows exemplars as small highlighted stars on a graph. Hover over one to see its trace ID.</Point>
  <Point label="The trace view">The trace view shows the root span's name and the trace ID at the top. Below it, each row is a span with its service, its operation and a bar for its duration.</Point>
  <Point label="Trace to logs">In this lab, a span offers a "Logs for this span" link. It searches Loki for lines of the same service that carry the trace's ID, a few minutes either side of the span.</Point>
  <Point label="Log back to trace">Each shop log line in Loki carries the trace ID as a field. The lab adds an "Open trace" link to that field, so you can go back to Tempo.</Point>
</Points>

<Do>
1. Open the dashboard <LabLink to="grafana" path="/d/shop-red-checkout">Shop / checkout (RED)</LabLink> in Grafana and set the time range to the last 15 minutes. The load generator places orders all the time, so the panels have data.
2. Read the three panels. Rate is requests per second, Errors is the share of failed spans, Duration is latency at p50, p95 and p99. All three come from Mimir.
3. In the Duration panel, hover over one of the highlighted stars near the p99 line. The tooltip shows a `traceID`. Click **Query with Tempo** next to it.
4. The trace opens in Explore. Read the trace ID in the header and copy it. Find the rows of checkout, cart, product-catalog and payment, and see which bar is the longest.
5. Click a span of the checkout service. In its details, look at the resource attributes and find `service.name`.
6. In the same span details, click **Logs for this span**. Loki opens beside the trace with a query on `service_name` and `trace_id`.
7. Expand one log line. Check that its `trace_id` field is the ID you copied, and that the field has an **Open trace** link.
8. Paste the trace ID in the lab check below.
</Do>

<Done>
You opened one trace from a star on the Duration panel. The log lines Loki showed all carry the same trace ID as the trace header.
</Done>

<Trap>
If "Logs for this span" shows no lines, the span you picked may belong to a service that wrote no log during this request. Pick a span of another service, such as payment, and try again. Do not search Loki by time alone: you would see the lines of many requests mixed together.
</Trap>

<Question id="q-m02-s06" answer="a">
A latency graph has a spike at 14:02. What does an exemplar on that spike give you?

- The trace ID of one request from that interval

  An exemplar links a metric value to one example trace.

- The average latency of all requests in that minute

  The graph line already shows the latency. The exemplar points to one request.

- Every log line written during the spike

  Logs are reached from the trace by its ID. The exemplar itself holds a trace ID.

- A list of all traces slower than the p99

  An exemplar is one example, not a list of all slow traces.
</Question>
