<Why>
Developers want to ship new features every day. Operations want fewer changes, because changes break things. Without a shared number for "reliable enough", every release turns into an argument. Users, too, need to know what they can expect from the service.
</Why>

<Idea>
An SLI measures how good the service is for users. An SLO is the target for that measure. An SLA is a contract that says what happens when the target is missed. The error budget is the failure the SLO still allows, and it decides when to slow down releases.
</Idea>

<Points>
  <Point label="SLI: the measure">A service level indicator is a carefully defined number about the service, such as the share of requests that succeed, or request latency. A good SLI measures the service as users see it.</Point>
  <Point label="SLO: the target">A service level objective is a target for an SLI over a time window. Example: 99.9% of checkout requests succeed over four weeks.</Point>
  <Point label="SLA: the contract">A service level agreement is a contract with users that includes consequences, often money back, when an SLO is missed. Ask "what happens if we miss it?". If nothing happens, it is an SLO, not an SLA.</Point>
  <Point label="Error budget: what may fail">The error budget is 100% minus the SLO. With a 99.9% SLO and 3 million requests in four weeks, 3,000 requests may fail. An outage that fails 1,500 requests uses half the budget.</Point>
  <Point label="What the budget decides">An error budget policy says what the team does when the budget runs out, for example: stop releases except urgent fixes until the service is back within its SLO. The budget turns the argument into a rule.</Point>
  <Point label="Why not 100%">A target of 100% is unrealistic and blocks change. Users would not see 100% anyway: the networks and devices between them and you fail too. So each extra nine costs more and helps users less.</Point>
</Points>

<Trap>
People often say "SLA" when they mean SLO. An internal target with no penalty is an SLO. Keep the two apart in interviews and in the exam.
</Trap>

<Question id="q-m02-s08" answer="b">
A service has an availability SLO of 99.5% over 30 days and served 2,000,000 requests in that window. How many failed requests does the error budget allow?

- 1,000

  That would be a 99.95% SLO. The budget is 0.5% of the requests.

- 10,000

  The budget is 100% minus 99.5%, so 0.5% of 2,000,000 requests.

- 99,500

  That mixes up the SLO percentage with a number of requests.

- 0, because an SLO allows no failures

  An SLO below 100% always leaves an error budget.
</Question>
