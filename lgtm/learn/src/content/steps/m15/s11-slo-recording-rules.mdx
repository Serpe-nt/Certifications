<Problem>
A platform team runs SLO alerts for 60 services, each with four windows. Written as raw queries, the 6-hour and 3-day windows read every span counter of every service once a minute, and the SLO dashboards read them again on every refresh. The rules work in a demo and slow the metrics store down in production.
</Problem>

<Idea>
Record each window's error ratio once per evaluation, under a name of its own, and let the alerts and dashboards read the recorded series. One recording rule per window per SLO, aggregated as two sums, is the shape the SRE workbook's own examples use.
</Idea>

## One recorded ratio per window

The workbook writes its alerts against series such as `job:slo_errors_per_request:ratio_rate1h`, and computes them with recording rules: the sum of the error rate divided by the sum of the request rate. Your group of step 10 does the same for four windows: `ratio_rate5m`, `ratio_rate30m`, `ratio_rate1h` and `ratio_rate6h`. The two alerts only compare four small series with thresholds.

The names keep the colons of `level:metric:operations` from module m04 step 12, loosely. The guidelines want the level to name the labels left and the metric to be the source metric unchanged. Here the level `learn_m15` marks the module's own series, which has no labels left, `checkout_errors` names what is counted, and `ratio_rate5m` says how. Real SLO rule sets bend the pattern the same way, as the workbook's `job:slo_errors_per_request:ratio_rate1h` does.

  <Layer title="ratio_rate5m, ratio_rate30m">Short windows, read by the two alerts.</Layer>
  <Layer title="ratio_rate1h, ratio_rate6h">Long windows, read by the alerts and by dashboards.</Layer>

## Order inside a group

The rules of a group run one after another at the same evaluation time, so an alert later in the group reads what the recording rules above it just wrote. In Mimir with ingest storage, a fresh sample first goes through Kafka; to read it, the ruler marks queries that depend on earlier rules of the same group for strong read consistency. Each ingester then waits until it has consumed everything written before the query. That is why, in the lab, each alert of the group takes about 2 seconds while each recording rule takes about 0.05.

## Ratios stay ratios of sums

A recorded ratio cannot be averaged into a longer window. The average of 288 five-minute ratios is not the 1-day ratio, because quiet five minutes weigh as much as busy ones. For each window you want, record the ratio of the two sums over that window, as the group does.

<InLab>
The span metrics of checkout are only two series for `PlaceOrder`, so in the lab a raw 6-hour query answers in a few hundredths of a second, as fast as the recorded one. The cost shows at scale. What the lab does show is the evaluation time of each rule, in the ruler's API.
</InLab>

<Remember>
One recording rule per window per SLO, each a ratio of two sums, named `level:metric:operations`. Alerts and dashboards read the recorded series; dependent rules in one Mimir group wait for strong consistency.
</Remember>

<Question id="q-m15-s11" answer="b">
A team records `slo:errors:ratio_rate5m` and builds a 30-day budget panel as `avg_over_time(slo:errors:ratio_rate5m[30d])`. Traffic is ten times higher by day than by night. What is wrong with the panel?

- Nothing, because the average of the 5-minute ratios is the 30-day ratio

  That holds only if every 5 minutes carry the same traffic, which is not the case here.

- Night ratios weigh as much as day ratios, so the result is not the 30-day error ratio

  Averaging ratios ignores how many requests each one covers. Record the 30-day ratio as a ratio of sums instead.

- avg_over_time cannot read a recorded series

  Recorded series are ordinary series; every function can read them.

- The panel shows the burn rate, not the error ratio

  The average of an error ratio is still an error ratio, only a wrongly weighted one.
</Question>
