<Problem>
A week after the payment incident of step 7, a colleague asks what happened. You remember "something with payment". The dashboards have moved on, the traces have been deleted by retention, and the person who switched the fault off is on holiday. What is left is whatever someone wrote down.
</Problem>

<Idea>
An incident note is the written record of one incident: what users felt and for how long, a timeline, the cause, the evidence that proves it, and what was done. Write it from the facts you collected during the investigation, with times, queries and IDs, so that someone who was not there can check every line.
</Idea>

## What goes into the note

Google's SRE book calls the written account of an incident a postmortem. It says what users lost, how the team stopped the damage, what caused it, and what will be done so that it does not happen again. A note for a small incident has the same parts, only shorter. Start with one sentence of summary. Then the impact: who was affected, how many, and for how long. Then the timeline, the cause, the evidence and the actions.

## The timeline is built from your evidence

Each line of a timeline has a time and one fact: when the fault started, when the first symptom showed, when someone noticed, when the cause was found, when it was fixed, when the graphs were back to normal. Take the times from the data, not from memory: the first failed span in Tempo, the first error log line, the minute the error ratio rose. The gaps between the lines are useful too. The time from the start to the first alert or complaint is the time to detect, and it often shows what to improve.

## Cause and evidence are not the same line

The cause is a statement: "the payment service refused charges with Invalid token". The evidence is what proves it, written so that someone else can find it again: the TraceQL query, one trace ID, the log line, and the count of failed orders. Say also what you ruled out and how, for example that the loyalty level on the failed spans was a coincidence because successful charges carry it too. A note that states a cause without evidence is an opinion.

## Blameless, and about the system

The SRE book asks for blameless postmortems: they look for the reasons in systems and processes, and assume that everyone acted in good faith with what they knew. So write "the fault flag stayed on for four minutes" rather than who forgot it. The actions at the end follow from that: what would have shown the fault sooner, what would have limited it, and who does each item. For the lab, one action might be an alert on the error ratio of checkout's server spans, which module m15 builds.

<Trap>
A note that only says what was done ("restarted payment, works now") is not finished. Without the cause and its evidence, nobody can tell whether the restart fixed anything or the fault just went away.
</Trap>

<Remember>
Summary, impact, timeline, cause, evidence, actions. Every time and every claim in the note points to data someone else can open.
</Remember>

<Question id="q-m14-s12" answer="c">
An incident note says: "14:05 checkout errors. 14:20 restarted payment. 14:21 fixed." What is the most important part missing?

- The names of the engineers who worked on it

  A blameless note is about the system. Names do not help the next reader find the cause.

- A screenshot of the dashboard during the incident

  A screenshot can support the evidence, but the note still does not say what the cause was.

- The cause and the evidence that proves it

  Without them, nobody knows whether the restart fixed the cause or the fault ended on its own.

- The exact minute the incident started, to the second

  More precise times help, but the note has no cause or proof at all, which matters more.
</Question>
