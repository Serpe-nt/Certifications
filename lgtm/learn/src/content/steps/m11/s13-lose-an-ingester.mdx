<Problem>
A node is drained for a kernel update and takes an ingester with it. Kafka is fine, the distributors are fine, the other two ingesters are fine. Do senders get errors? Do dashboards go blank, or show a hole afterwards? You will delete one ingester of the lab and watch.
</Problem>

<Idea>
With ingest storage, losing an ingester never stops writes: they end at Kafka. Reads of recent data need one healthy ingester per partition, so a partition with only one ingester fails its queries until that ingester is back. It then replays its WAL and catches up from its Kafka offset, and no sample is lost. Two ingesters per partition, in different zones, keep reads running too.
</Idea>

## Writes go on

The distributors write to Kafka, and Kafka replicates each partition across its brokers. No ingester takes part in a write, so a missing ingester changes nothing for the senders. The records of its partition simply wait in Kafka.

## Reads need one ingester per partition

A querier asks one ingester of each partition. All ingesters of a partition read the same records in the same order, so one answer is enough: a read quorum of one. The documentation calls the read path healthy as long as every partition has at least one healthy ingester, and recommends two ingesters per partition, a replication factor of two.

With one ingester per partition, losing it leaves its partition with no reader. A query that needs recent data then fails with an error that names the partition, until an ingester is back. A query that reaches only the store-gateways, for data older than 13 hours, does not ask the ingesters at all.

## Catching up without a gap

The new ingester replays its WAL, then reads its partition from the offset stored in its consumer group. It tries to bring its lag under 2 seconds, and it must be under 15 seconds before it turns `ACTIVE` in the ring and serves queries again; both are the defaults. Because Kafka kept every record, the series have no hole afterwards.

## Zones

Zone-aware replication puts the ingesters of one partition in different zones, such as availability zones or racks. With ingest storage there is no replication factor to set: you run two ingesters per partition, mark each with its zone, and switch zone awareness on for distributors, ingesters and queriers. Then a whole zone can go down and every partition still has a reader. The documentation also warns that rollouts must update one zone at a time, a job for Grafana's rollout-operator.

<Compare caption="Losing one ingester">
  <Col title="Classic, replication factor 3">Writes and reads need a quorum of 2 of 3 ingesters per series; a restarted ingester has a gap for the time it was down.</Col>
  <Col title="Ingest storage, one ingester per partition">Writes go on; recent reads of its partition fail until it is back; no gap.</Col>
  <Col title="Ingest storage, two zones" hot>Writes go on; reads use the other zone's ingester; no gap.</Col>
</Compare>

</InLab>

<Remember>
Ingest storage: an ingester down stops no write, and Kafka fills the gap when it returns. Recent reads need one healthy ingester per partition, so run two per partition in different zones.
</Remember>

<Question id="q-m11-s13" answer="d">
A Mimir cluster with ingest storage runs two zones, with one ingester per partition in each zone. Zone A loses all its ingesters at once for ten minutes. What happens?

- Writes fail, because half of the ingesters are gone

  Ingesters are not on the write path. Writes end at Kafka.

- Queries of recent data fail until zone A is back

  Every partition still has its zone B ingester, and one per partition is enough.

- Zone A's ingesters lose the samples of those ten minutes

  They read their partitions from their stored offsets when they return, so they catch up without a gap.

- Writes and queries both go on, and zone A catches up from Kafka later

  Kafka takes the writes, zone B serves the reads, and zone A resumes from its consumer group offsets.
</Question>
